About

For the past five years I have built the Higher Ed and Financial Aid platforms at The College Board, products that millions of students around the world depend on. I set architectural direction for those products across multiple teams of four to six engineers, including the modernization of a large legacy product on AWS. That means reviewing the lead engineers' designs, deciding technology trade-offs with principal architects, and aligning the roadmap with product.

The systems I design are distributed and serverless: AWS Lambda, API Gateway and DynamoDB, defined in TypeScript with AWS CDK and shipped through automated pipelines. Recent work includes a common library that improved REST API P99 latency by more than 60% across all microservices, and a batch-processing library, adopted by multiple teams, for runs of up to hundreds of thousands of students. I care about what keeps a system healthy after launch: observability, cost, failure modes, and the runbooks that let a team sleep.

I still write production code every week. Most recently I have contributed to AI initiatives on those platforms, including OCR document processing, an AI chatbot, and AI evaluations. Before this I built financial and education products as a full-stack engineer, and I started my career writing C++ for CAD software. I hold a Master's in Software Engineering from George Mason University.
